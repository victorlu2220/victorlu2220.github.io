TactileArena: the Ground as a Sensor

Scalable high-density tactile arenas for measuring ground-contact forces in freely moving animals

The idea: Seen from above, a walking rat hides its paws under its body — video and pose tracking can say where the body is and how it moves, but not how much weight each paw carries. TactileArena puts the measurement where the animal meets the world: the floor itself is a dense grid of pressure sensors (taxels), so every contact appears as an image of the paw pressing down, frame by frame. The overhead camera supplies the body's position and orientation, and the floor supplies the forces. In one recording you get the familiar gait measures (when each paw lands, how long it stays down) plus what video cannot see: how hard each paw pushes, how weight shifts between paws, and how load moves across a single paw during a step.

16,384 taxels, from four 64×64 modules
2 mm taxel pitch — resolves a rat's paw print
~1 kHz nominal frame rate
4.9% of taxels read per frame — the rest is skipped until something presses

Why it matters

We tracked rats for two weeks after a pinch injury to the left sciatic nerve (three injured, two sham-operated). In the first days after surgery, the injured animals kept placing the injured hind paw on the ground — timing alone showed a moderate deficit, but the loading measured by the floor showed a much larger one. The hindpaw's share of stance time fell by about a third, while its share of load fell by about 60%. Both ratios moved back toward baseline by days 11–13.

−0.33 change in timing ratio (days 1–5)
−0.61 change in loading ratio (days 1–5)

How it works

Large, dense, fast pressure-sensing surfaces usually force a trade-off between area, resolution, and speed. TactileArena avoids it by reading each taxel only when it is likely to matter.

  • Sense: each module is a flex circuit with a 64×64 electrode grid under a piezoresistive film, covered by a waterproof cloth; four modules tile the 512 mm runway.
  • Sample: each board reads 200 of its 4,096 taxels per frame via a coarse background scan; any reading above threshold triggers full-resolution reads of its neighbors.
  • Fuse: an overhead camera on the same clock supplies body orientation; contacts are then assigned to paws with 93.3% accuracy against 2,469 hand-labeled contacts.
